Mariner's Chowder

My grandfather first made this chowder on a dragger out of New Bedford, where the cook's whole job was to make cold men warm again. He measured nothing and burned nothing, and the recipe below is my best translation of watching him not explain anything for thirty years.

The trick, if there is one, is patience with the salt pork and cowardice with the heat once the cream goes in. A chowder that boils is a chowder you start over — the cream breaks, the cod toughens, and the watch below goes unfed. Keep it at a shudder, never a bubble, and it will forgive nearly anything else you do to it.

Serve it the day you make it if you must, but it is better the second day, once the potatoes have given up their starch and the broth has thickened to the weight of the fog it was invented in.
